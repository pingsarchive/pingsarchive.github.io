archiveDescription: A 1970 gouache on paper by Tatiana Bystrova, combining
  luminous geometric structures with an expansive dark field and small human
  figures. Networks of fine white lines radiate between points of light and
  translucent orange, pink, blue, and violet forms, giving the composition the
  appearance of a diagram suspended in cosmic space. Bystrova was associated
  with Dvizhenie, the Moscow experimental art group whose work explored
  geometry, movement, light, and the visual language of scientific and
  technological research.
reason: the difference in scale between the system above and the figures beneath
  it. the lines make something invisible feel diagrammable — as if space, light,
  gravity, or communication could actually be mapped — while the people at the
  bottom make the entirety suddenly feel bodily and immense.

Signed, titled, inscribed “Moskva” in Cyrillic and dated 1970 on the reverse. Provenance: the artist’s studio; Lev Nussberg, until 1972. Exhibited in *Nonkonformisty: vtoroy russkiy avangard 1955–1988* at the State Russian Museum, St Petersburg; State Tretyakov Gallery, Moscow; Städel, Frankfurt, and other venues, 1996–97; and *Avantgarde im Untergrund* at Kunstmuseum Bern, 2005.
