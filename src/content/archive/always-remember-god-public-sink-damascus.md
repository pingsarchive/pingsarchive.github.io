A public sink documented in Damascus, Syria, bearing the inscription **اذكر الله** (*udhkur Allah*, “Remember God”) and the English translation **“ALWAYS REMEMBER GOD.”**

Ethnomusicologist Victor A. Vicente encountered the sink while walking through Damascus on October 4, 2004. He described it as a simple public sink placed beside the road for travelers and reproduced a photograph of it as “Figure 53: Public Sink.”

The Arabic imperative **اذكر الله** literally means “remember God” or “remember Allah.” It invokes the Islamic concept of **dhikr** (*zikr*), remembrance or continual awareness of God. Vicente later explains dhikr as encompassing both memory and invocation, and notes that reminders of God can appear throughout everyday life in forms ranging from spoken expressions to signs and inscriptions.

Here, that devotional instruction is incorporated into a mundane public utility: drinking water. The sink turns an ordinary pause on a city street into a moment of remembrance.
