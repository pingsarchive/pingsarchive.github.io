De Saussure developed the cyanometer as a means of assigning numerical values to variations in the blue of the sky. The observer compared the sky directly with numbered painted samples arranged around the instrument. His earlier cyanometers used fewer shades; later circular versions expanded the scale substantially, extending between white and black. **[[Musée d’histoire des sciences, Geneva ↗]](https://institutions.ville-geneve.ch/fileadmin/user_upload/mhn/documents/Musee_histoire_des_sciences/aide_saussure.pdf)**

De Saussure used cyanometers during Alpine observations, comparing readings taken at different elevations. Geneva museum documentation describes a circular version taken to the Col du Géant and comparative observations made at the Col, Chamonix, and Geneva. His experiments were part of a broader attempt to understand atmospheric transparency and the relationship between sky color and matter suspended in the air. **[[Musée d’histoire des sciences, Geneva ↗]](https://institutions.ville-geneve.ch/fileadmin/user_upload/mhn/documents/Musee_histoire_des_sciences/aide_saussure.pdf)**

In 1790, de Saussure presented *Description d'un Cyanomètre ou d'un Appareil Destiné à Mesurer l'Intensité de la Couleur Bleue du Ciel* to the Académie Royale des Sciences de Turin. The published illustration was simplified; surviving documentation distinguishes it from the more finely graduated instruments actually used in observation. **[[Accademia delle Scienze di Torino ↗]](https://www.accademiadellescienze.it/media/18bdb166-4a81-4622-a43f-3b7891451e12) [[1790 cyanometer plate ↗]](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AMemoires_de_l%27Academie_Royale_des_Sciences_%281790%29_-_de_Saussure%27s_Cyanometer.jpg)**
